Real Wedding: A Palace Celebration in Udaipur

Inside a three-day Udaipur wedding, from a lakeside mehendi to the final farewell at dawn.

Humsafar Wedding Editorial January 2026 · 8 min read

Inside the Three Days

  1. Day one: mehendi by the lake
  2. Day two: sangeet in the courtyard
  3. Day three: the wedding ceremony
  4. What made this wedding work

This three-day Udaipur wedding is one we still refer back to internally when we're training new members of our planning team — not because anything went dramatically wrong, but because everything came together exactly the way careful planning is supposed to make it look effortless. The family had chosen a heritage palace on Lake Pichola for all three functions, which meant our design brief wasn't to build a set from scratch but to work with — and around — four centuries of architecture that was never going to need much help looking beautiful.

Day One: Mehendi by the Lake

The family wanted an unfussy first day, so we set up floor seating on a lakeside terrace with minimal structural décor, letting the palace's own carved stonework and the lake view carry the visual weight while the mehendi artists worked through the afternoon. Rather than a single large canopy, we used a scatter of low, cushioned seating clusters shaded by simple cotton umbrellas, so guests could move between conversations instead of being locked into rows. A live santoor player performed through the afternoon at low volume — present, but never competing with the sound of the lake.

Day Two: Sangeet in the Courtyard

A restored inner courtyard became the sangeet venue, with lighting design doing most of the transformation work after sunset — the same space that had hosted a quiet afternoon tea for elderly guests became an entirely different atmosphere by evening, warmed by amber uplighting against the sandstone walls and a specially built stage sized to the courtyard's own proportions rather than a standard rig. Rehearsed performances from both families ran until nearly midnight, and our team kept a rolling buffet moving through the courtyard so no one had to leave the dance floor to eat.

"The brief for this wedding was never to build a set. It was to make sure four centuries of architecture never had to compete with anything we added." Humsafar Wedding Design Team

Day Three: The Wedding Ceremony

The couple chose an early-morning ceremony to avoid the midday desert heat, which meant our on-ground team began setup well before sunrise, working by torchlight and headlamp to dress the mandap in fresh marigold and white jasmine garlands before the first guests arrived. By the time the family reached the courtyard, the mandap was fully dressed and the palace was catching the first light across the lake exactly as we'd planned it — a detail no one but our team had spent the previous evening worrying about, and exactly the kind of detail that makes a wedding morning feel unhurried instead of rushed.

What Made This Wedding Work

Looking back at the run sheets afterward, a few decisions stand out as the ones that quietly made everything else possible. None of them were expensive. All of them took planning further in advance than most families think to ask for.

What Made This Wedding Work

  • A day-by-day design plan matched to each event's actual mood, not one theme stretched across three days
  • Pre-dawn setup calls timed specifically around the desert heat, not a generic schedule
  • Letting the palace architecture lead the design, rather than competing with it
  • A dedicated on-ground team briefed on the family's specific rituals well in advance
  • A rolling buffet at the sangeet so no guest had to choose between dancing and eating

Three days, one palace, and a family who trusted us with the details — that combination is, more often than not, exactly the recipe behind the weddings we're proudest of. Udaipur gives a planning team an unusual amount to work with: water, stone, and light that changes completely between morning and evening. Our job on this one was mostly to get out of its way.
